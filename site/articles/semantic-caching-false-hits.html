Semantic Caching for LLMs: Avoiding Wrong Cached Answers

By Nawal · 2026-10-21 · 7 min read

A semantic cache stores LLM answers keyed by the embedding of the question. When a new question's embedding is close enough to a stored one, the stored answer is returned and no model call is made. For a RAG product with repetitive traffic, this is the single largest cost lever. In our benchmark the hit rate was 70%, against 26% for an exact-match cache.

It is also the easiest way to quietly ship wrong answers. A cache that checks similarity alone served another question's answer on 21% of its hits, and overall accuracy dropped 15.7 points. This article explains why that happens and the four checks that brought false hits down to 0.1%.

Why similarity alone is not enough

Embeddings are built to put sentences with similar wording and topic close together. Caching needs something stricter: questions that have the same answer. Those diverge in three common ways.

  1. Entity swaps. "How much storage does the Pro plan include?" and "How much storage does the Business plan include?" differ by a single token. Their cosine similarity is very high, but the answers are 1,000 GB and 3,000 GB.
  2. Number swaps. "What would 12 seats cost per year?" and "What would 30 seats cost per year?" also differ by one token, and the answers differ.
  3. Filler inflation. Real users write "Hey team, quick question: … Thanks in advance!" When two different short questions share the same greeting and sign-off, the shared filler can push their similarity above any sensible threshold.

Raising the threshold helps, but it gives up hits on real paraphrases and never fully removes entity swaps, because those are often the most similar pairs of all.

Four checks for a cache hit

CheckWhat it comparesCatches
1. Similaritycosine(query embeddings) ≥ thresholdunrelated questions
2. Key termsnumbers and entity names must match exactlyentity and number swaps
3. Topic overlapJaccard over query words found in the knowledge basefiller inflation, attribute swaps
4. Context overlapJaccard over retrieved document idsanswers grounded in different docs

Key terms, learned from your corpus

You don't need a hand-maintained entity list. Scan the knowledge base for words that appear capitalised mid-sentence (product names, plan names, feature names). Drop the ones that appear in most documents, such as the product's own name, because they don't tell answers apart. Add every number in the query. Two questions may share a cached answer only if these sets are identical.

Topic overlap ignores small talk

Take the query words that also occur in the knowledge base, minus stopwords. "Thanks", "team" and "proposal" never appear in product docs, so they drop out. "Storage", "history" and "rate limit" do appear, so they count. Requiring a Jaccard overlap of at least 0.6 rejects "Pro vs Business on storage" against "… on version history", however much filler the two share.

Context overlap keeps answers grounded

Retrieval already runs before the cache lookup, because the router needs its scores too. Storing the retrieved document ids with each cache entry is free. If a new query retrieves different documents, the cached answer was grounded in different evidence and should not be reused. This check also invalidates naturally when documents change.

The guarded cache keeps most of the naive cache's savings: cost falls 72% instead of 79%, with quality at the baseline level. The guards cost microseconds, since they are set comparisons on a handful of tokens.

Practical advice

  • Measure false hits, not just hit rate. A hit-rate dashboard rewards exactly the failure described here. Sample cache hits and check whether the matched question has the same answer.
  • Start the threshold around 0.8 and sweep it. With guards in place, you can lower it for more hits. Our threshold sweep stays under 0.5% false hits even at 0.6.
  • Remember the cache freezes answers. If a wrong answer gets cached, it is served until it expires. Use a TTL, and consider caching only answers from your strongest model.
  • Use a real sentence-embedding model in production. It catches true paraphrases that lexical embedders miss. That raises similarity for look-alikes too, which makes the guards even more important.
