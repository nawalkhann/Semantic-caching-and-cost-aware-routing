What the numbers say

  • Naive semantic caching hurts quality. With similarity only, 21% of cache hits returned another question's answer, and accuracy fell 15.7 points. Look-alike questions such as "Pro vs Business on storage" and "Pro vs Business on version history" embed very closely, especially when users add greetings and sign-offs.
  • Guards fix it cheaply. Requiring matching entities and numbers, overlapping topic words and the same retrieved documents cuts false hits to 0.1%. The hit rate stays at 70%, compared with 26% for an exact-match cache.
  • Routing alone cuts cost but not tail latency. The router saves 32% of cost, but p95 moves only 1%%. The slowest requests are the hard ones, and those correctly still go to the large model.
  • Combined, cost per request falls 83% and p95 latency 33%. Accuracy is 96.0% vs 96.2%, inside the seed-to-seed noise.

Cache similarity threshold

Lower thresholds give more hits and lower cost. Because the guards catch look-alikes, false hits stay under 0.5% even at 0.6. Accuracy still becomes noisier at low thresholds, because a wrong answer that gets cached is then reused more often.

How the benchmark works

Real: retrieval, embeddings, the cache and its guards, the router, token counts for every prompt, local compute latency, and grading against reference answers.

Simulated: the LLM calls. Each tier has an explicit price, latency profile and accuracy per difficulty. The simulated model answers correctly with that probability and otherwise gives a plausible wrong answer.

Small modelLarge model
Price, USD per 1M tokens (in / out)0.15 / 0.603.00 / 15.00
Accuracy, easy / hard questions96% / 62%98% / 94%
Time to first token / per output token0.30 s / 8 ms0.80 s / 25 ms

The workload is a fictional SaaS product's documentation: 9 documents and 73 question intents, each with 3–5 paraphrases. Requests are drawn with Zipf popularity, and users add greetings, sign-offs, casing changes and word swaps. Hit rates depend on how repetitive traffic is, so expect lower hit rates on long-tail traffic. Accuracy differences under about 1 point are within noise.

Reproduce it with python benchmarks/run_benchmark.py. The raw results are in results.json.
